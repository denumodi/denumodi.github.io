Understanding Occupational Burnout vs. Transient Fatigue

In organizational psychology, occupational burnout (as formalized by Christina Maslach’s tri-factor framework) is distinct from ordinary end-of-day tiredness. Acute fatigue resolves after a single night of restorative sleep or a quiet weekend. Cumulative burnout, by contrast, develops when high cognitive load and frequent task interruptions outpace physiological recovery over multiple consecutive weeks, producing three measurable shifts: chronic emotional exhaustion, psychological detachment from work meaning, and reduced perceived efficacy.
