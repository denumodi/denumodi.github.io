Scientific Methodology: How the 2D Circumplex Mood Check Works

Most casual mood trackers force human experience into a single one-dimensional scale from “bad” to “good.” In affective neuroscience and psychometrics, however, a single axis fails to distinguish between states that share the same pleasantness but differ dramatically in physiological activation. For example, feeling serene and feeling exhilarated are both positive mood states, yet they require completely different cognitive workflows. Similarly, anxious overload and exhausted apathy are both unpleasant, but an intervention that helps exhaustion (such as brisk movement or bright light exposure) can worsen acute anxiety.

The DenuModi Mood Check & Emotional Energy Analyzer implements psychologist James A. Russell’s landmark Circumplex Model of Affect (1980) combined with the PANAS (Positive and Negative Affect Schedule) and modern circadian sleep-wake homeostasis models. By evaluating five core internal sliders alongside four physiological modifiers (sleep duration, caffeine pharmacokinetics, unbroken screen exposure, and hydration status), the tool maps your current state into one of four actionable affective quadrants.

Why Sleep Debt, Caffeine, and Screen Duration Shift Your Baseline Mood

Self-reported mood scores do not exist in a vacuum; they are heavily modulated by biological homeostats. Research in chronobiology demonstrates two primary processes governing daytime mood stability: Process S (homeostatic sleep pressure), driven by the gradual accumulation of extracellular adenosine in the basal forebrain, and Process C (circadian pacemaker signaling), regulated by the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN).

Key Psychometric Insight: When sleep duration drops below 6.5 hours, functional connectivity between the ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) and the amygdala decreases significantly, amplifying emotional reactivity to minor workplace friction by up to 60% while reducing working memory capacity.

Furthermore, consuming more than 200 mg of caffeine on top of high sleep debt blocks adenosine A1 and A2A receptors temporarily without clearing the underlying metabolic fatigue. Once caffeine plasma concentration begins its half-life decay (typically 4 to 6 hours post-ingestion), accumulated adenosine binds rapidly, triggering a steep afternoon drop in both emotional valence and cognitive clarity—a phenomenon known clinically as the adenosine rebound crash.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. How often should I perform a structured mood check?
Checking in two to three times per day—typically 60 minutes after waking, mid-afternoon (around 2:30 PM), and at the end of your workday—captures your circadian peak, post-prandial dip, and cumulative cognitive load without causing tracking fatigue.
2. Is my mood check data uploaded to any cloud server?
No. DenuModi processes all slider values, lexical journal analysis, and historical logs 100% locally inside your web browser using JavaScript and localStorage. You can export your log as a CSV file or clear it permanently at any time.
3. What is the difference between Emotional Valence and Arousal?
Valence measures how pleasant or unpleasant a feeling is (horizontal axis), while Arousal measures the degree of physiological activation, alertness, or autonomic energy accompanying that state (vertical axis). Combining both reveals whether a low mood is tense/overwhelmed or sluggish/depleted.
4. Can this tool diagnose clinical depression or anxiety disorders?
No. DenuModi is an educational self-reflection, circadian energy, and workplace productivity utility. It is not a medical device or psychiatric diagnostic instrument. If you experience persistent distress, please consult a licensed healthcare professional.
